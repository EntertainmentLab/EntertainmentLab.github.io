Diagnosis

Why the panel saw 20% incidence

After the soft launch, the panel reported an unexpectedly low 20% incidence rate, 243 screener terminations against seven quality terminations and a dropout rate above 30%, and paused data collection. Reconciling the panel’s dispositions with the survey’s own records accounts for every exit — and neither screening nor the attention check is the cause.

The 243 exits the panel logged as screener terminations are not a screener. The survey requires a desktop browser because of its microphone questions and poster grids, and it redirects phone and tablet users out with the screening disposition code the moment the page loads — before a single response is saved. Those exits are the panel’s ordinary mobile traffic, about 54% of clicks. The booked incidence of roughly 22% is an arithmetic consequence: 70 completes divided by 320 completes-plus-terminations.

The dropout the panel flagged is the voice verification, confirmed in the response records. Of 210 desktop starters, 120 never attempted the microphone and 8 tried but failed, so 128 of the 133 true dropouts — 96% — happened on the landing page. The attention check terminated just 7, about 9% of the 82 who entered the survey proper. Once inside, panelists finished: 70 of the 82 who entered completed (85%), with no speeder flags.

Two implications follow. First, yield is predictable rather than broken: about 15% of raw clicks become completes, so a 200-complete target costs roughly 1,300 clicks. Second, the surviving sample is desktop-owning, microphone-granting panelists, a selection that can correlate with age, income and comfort with technology, so demographic quotas should be enforced on completes rather than invites. Routing only desktop users into the study on the panel side would eliminate the wasted mobile invites and normalize the booked incidence without touching the instrument.

The click total is estimated as desktop entries plus panel-reported device redirects; the panel’s own click count may differ slightly. The mic requirement is integral to the instrument — several later questions are voice-first — so relaxing it is an instrument decision, not a fielding one.
