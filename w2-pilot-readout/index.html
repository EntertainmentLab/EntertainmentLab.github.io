01

Overview

Pilot 2 is the first field on the 79-question rotation-era instrument (published Sep 1, 19:46 UTC, source 8d2c3b17): phones and tablets eligible for the first time, landing-page telemetry active, the gate’s audio retained, and the five ungated voice probes rotating 2-of-5. Data below runs through Sep 02, 2026 13:45 UTC and is still accruing toward the ~100-complete ask.

Field notes in one paragraph. The mobile change did what it was supposed to: nearly three-quarters of traffic arrived on phones, phones out-passed desktops at the spoken gate (73% vs 63% of arrivals ever passing), and the funnel converted half of all arrivals into completes — against a third in pilot 1 on a shorter instrument. The 2-of-5 rotation drew within sampling noise of even at arrival level, every shown open item was answered, and roughly half of those answers came in by voice. The main quality caveats are a speeder who finished in 1.7 minutes (real speech on the voice items, straight-line pace everywhere else), and session-reuse artifacts that make created_at and single-load telemetry unreliable for returning devices — both detailed in section 08.

03

Landing page: the full diagnostic picture

Pilot 1 could not tell a blocked microphone from an ignored one. The telemetry shipped for pilot 2 stamps every arrival with a capability probe and logs every tap, grant, denial and notice — so for the first time each of the 51 landing losses has an attributable cause.

capability probe

What arrivals brought to the page

Passive probe at page load: mic permission state, input devices, secure context. No permission prompt fires.

telemetrybase: 166 probed arrivals

147 of 166 arrivals (89%) had never been asked for their microphone — the gate is almost always the first ask. Only 2 arrived pre-denied. 3 arrivals reported no microphone hardware at all: automatic gate deaths that pilot 1 would have booked, unexplained, as screener terminations.

gate outcomes

Three ways to die at the gate, now countable

Every arrival ends the landing in one of three states.

mic gatebase: all 167 starters

The dominant loss is refusal to engage: 30 arrivals (18%) read the “say ‘I am human’” ask and left without one tap — concentrated on desktop. 21 tried and could not get through (permission blocks, transcription failures, no-mic hardware). Browser permission machinery itself was cooperative: 74 permission-state change events were recorded — typically the prompt-to-granted grant — and pre-denied browsers were nearly absent.

blocked taps

15 arrivals fought a blocked microphone

attempt_mic_blocked entries in the bot-check log — taps where the browser refused the mic stream. That covers both a participant tapping Don't-allow on the permission popup and a device- or browser-level block where no popup appears; the log records the refusal, not the reason. Logged per attempt since the Sep-1 fix.

telemetrybase: 15 arrivals with a blocked tap

Two distinct frictions separate cleanly in the log. Permission friction: 15 arrivals tapped and were refused — some declining the popup themselves, some facing a settings-level block (a post-refusal permission state of ‘denied’ marks the persistent blocks; ‘prompt’ usually marks a declined or dismissed popup). One entrant tapped 24 times before giving up — a pattern that fits a dead, hard-blocked button rather than 24 separate refusals. Transcription friction: 21 “couldn’t transcribe” notices — the mic granted, speech recorded, but STT failing to return the phrase; in the session timelines reviewed these cluster on phones, with participants retrying three to five times and some abandoning with a working microphone. That second group is the cheapest fixable loss at the gate.

Reloading the landing page resets the in-memory log, so blocked-tap counts undercount strugglers who reload; treat these as lower bounds.
