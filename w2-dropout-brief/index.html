The short answer

Three-quarters of all drop-outs happen on the very first page, before any survey question. The survey opens with a required spoken bot-check (“tap the mic and say: I am human”). Respondents who clear that page almost always finish: of the 194 who cleared it, 145 completed and 18 were quality-terminated by the attention check — only ~16% abandoned somewhere in the survey body, with no single section responsible.

What page one is, and why it filters

The landing page carries the study’s anti-bot design: an invisible check plus a required spoken phrase, because the study includes voice-recorded questions. Of the 124 entrants who died on page one:

80
never tapped the mic
read the ask, chose not to engage
44
tried but couldn’t get through
mic blocked, no mic, or speech not recognized

From Sep 3, ~02:15 UTC we shipped recovery paths for the second group (retry guidance, classified fallbacks after repeated technical failures). In the Sep 3 traffic, 26 entrants who would previously have been lost entered through these fallbacks and were surveyed. The first group — declining to speak — is inherent to a voice-required study and is best addressed upstream, in invite framing and targeting.

Why the last 24 hours looked worse (your 53%)

Completion is strongly device-dependent, and the two batches you sent differed sharply in device mix:

Phones complete at 50–52%; desktops at 35–41%. Desktops fail the spoken check far more often — many desktop machines have no working microphone, and desktop users engage the mic ask less. The Sep-3 batch was almost entirely desktop, which is why your last-24-hour drop-out figure spiked; nothing else about the survey changed for the worse in that window (the recovery fallbacks shipped mid-wave and helped).

If further fielding happens

Two levers on your side move completion materially: weight targeting toward mobile, and set the expectation in the invite that the study requires a microphone and includes questions answered aloud. On our side the recovery fallbacks are live, so purely technical microphone failures no longer end a session.

Reconciliation notes

Our “entrant” = a session row created at first page load carrying your rid parameter, so this includes people who bounced in seconds. Completes were returned to you as st=com; attention-check and speeder terminations as st=qct; bot detections as st=sec. Everyone else produced no exit redirect and will appear as a drop-out in your reconciliation — on our books that is 155 of 318 (49%) overall and 57% within the Sep-3 batch, consistent with the 53% you observed for your window.
